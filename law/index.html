Roy L. Pearson, Jr.

Defendants

Jin & Soo Chung (Custom Cleaners)

Small family-owned dry cleaning business

Statement of Facts

In May 2005, Roy Pearson dropped off a pair of suit trousers for alterations at Custom Cleaners in D.C. When he returned to pick them up, the shop was unable to locate the pants. A few days later, the shop found the trousers and tried to return them, but Pearson refused them, insisting they were not his.

Pointing to two prominent store signs that advertised "Satisfaction Guaranteed" and "Same Day Service," Pearson decided to file a massive lawsuit under the District of Columbia Consumer Protection Procedures Act.

Pearson's $67 Million Legal Formula:

  • $1,500 for the original cost of a replacement suit.
  • $500,000 in legal attorney fees (representing himself).
  • $2,000,000 for mental anguish and emotional distress.
  • $64,000,000 in statutory penalties for calculated daily violations of "Satisfaction Guaranteed" for 10 years.

Pearson argued that because the store failed to satisfy him, every single day the "Satisfaction Guaranteed" sign remained displayed in the window constituted a fraudulent statutory penalty against the public.

Actual Court Decision & Analysis

The Final Verdict & The Real Cost

D.C. Superior Court Ruling

Judge Judith Bartnoff ruled entirely in favor of the dry cleaners (the Chungs). The court rejected Pearson's claims, stating that marketing slogans like "Satisfaction Guaranteed" do not give a consumer unlimited rights to demand unreasonable remedies.

"A reasonable consumer would not interpret 'Satisfaction Guaranteed' to mean a store must pay millions for lost pants."
The Tragic Business Reality

Despite winning completely in court, the Chungs incurred over $100,000 in defense legal fees. Due to public pressure, emotional strain, and lost revenue, they closed Custom Cleaners permanently a short time later.

Aftermath: Roy Pearson lost his re-appointment as an administrative judge due to his lack of judicial restraint and was later suspended from practicing law.

Student Executive Reflection

Suppose you are advising a new retail business. Based on this case, write 2 specific legal or operational steps the business should take to protect itself from extreme customer litigation.

Reveal Instructor Key Takeaways & Mitigation Strategies

1. Precise Slogan Disclaimers: Add subtle qualifying language to marketing material (e.g., "See store for terms & limitations" or "Satisfaction guaranteed subject to store policy limits").

2. Business Liability Insurance: Maintain robust commercial general liability (CGL) coverage with litigation defense benefits to cover defense fees.

3. Business Structure & Fee-Shifting Contracts: Utilize liability limitation clauses in receipt terms (e.g., "Liability limited to garment value").

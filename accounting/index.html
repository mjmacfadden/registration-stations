The Scenario

Throughout the school year, Marcus served as Student Council President and oversaw all cash operations for the Friday Night Concession Stand across Football, Basketball, and Track meets.

At year-end, the school treasurer noticed that despite record-breaking attendance and sell-out crowds, the concession account barely broke even. Marcus claims that exorbitant supply costs, broken equipment, and frequent food spoilage consumed all the profits.

An audit of the Annual Concession Stand Statement of Cash Flows tells a very different story. Your goal: examine the cash flow statement to uncover how Marcus systematically skimmed cash into his personal pocket over the past 10 months.

The Forensic Debrief

1. The Monthly Skim

Direct Personal Transfers

Marcus paid himself a $4,000 "Transport Allowance" and took $6,000 in cash advances for school decorations that were never purchased, walking away with $10,000 straight into his pocket.

2. The "Ghost" Expenses

Padded Operational Outflows

He masked missing cash by fabricating $5,000 in handwritten "emergency grocery purchases" and $3,500 in cash refunds for "spoiled snacks" without disposal discrepancy logs ($8,500).

3. Eliminating Digital Records

Destroying The Paper Trail

Marcus liquidated the Square electronic card readers (+$1,000 cash) to force the concession stand to be 100% cash-only. No card records meant nobody could verify how much money actually entered the registers.

What Real Forensic Accountants Look For

Segregation of Duties Marcus handled the cash, bought the supplies, approved the payouts, and reported the balances. In business, the person handling cash should never be the person writing or approving the checks!
Itemized Substantiation Handwritten slips and vague claims of "food spoilage" are classic fraud red flags. Legitimate businesses require register receipts, vendor invoices, and dual-party inventory waste logs.

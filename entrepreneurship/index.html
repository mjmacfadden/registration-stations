Netflix Tries to Sell Itself to Blockbuster

The Giant

Blockbuster Inc. (CEO John Antioco)

~9,000 stores nationwide, roughly $5 billion in annual revenue

Statement of Facts

In 2000, fresh off the dot-com crash, tiny DVD-by-mail startup Netflix was burning cash and struggling to raise money. Co-founders Reed Hastings and Marc Randolph flew to Blockbuster's Dallas headquarters with a proposal: Netflix would run Blockbuster's online brand and operations, while Blockbuster promoted Netflix inside its thousands of physical stores.

The asking price for the partnership was $50 million — a figure Blockbuster, with billions in annual revenue and a dominant nationwide store footprint, could easily afford.

The Size Mismatch:

  • ~$5 million Netflix's total annual revenue in 2000.
  • ~$5 billion Blockbuster's annual revenue — roughly 1,000x larger.
  • 9,000+ Blockbuster physical retail stores nationwide.
  • $50 million Netflix's asking price for the entire partnership.

Blockbuster's leadership reportedly viewed Netflix's online, no-late-fee subscription model as a tiny, unprofitable niche business unworthy of serious attention.

What Actually Happened Next

From Laughed Out of the Room to a $150 Billion Company

Blockbuster Passed on the Deal

Confident in its thousands of stores and profitable late-fee revenue model, Blockbuster declined the partnership. Netflix kept growing, eventually eliminating late fees entirely — directly solving customers' #1 complaint about Blockbuster.

Netflix pivoted hard into streaming starting in 2007, years ahead of most competitors.
The Business Reality

Blockbuster filed for bankruptcy in September 2010, crushed by debt, store leases, and its failure to adapt. Only a single corporate-owned store remains today, run as a novelty. Netflix is now valued at over $150 billion.

Aftermath: This story is now a textbook example of the Innovator's Dilemma in entrepreneurship and strategy courses worldwide.
Final Step • Venture Strategy

Who Is Tomorrow's Blockbuster?

Blockbuster failed because leadership couldn't imagine a world where customers wouldn't drive to physical stores and pay late fees. As an entrepreneur, your job is to spot these blind spots before everyone else does.
